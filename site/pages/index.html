Features & controls.

A small overlay sits on top of your game with the live frame rate and what’s behind it: CPU and GPU load, temperatures and power draw, memory pressure, disk activity, fans and battery. It’s a native Apple silicon app under 10 MB.

Use the FPS preset for just the frame-rate number, Balanced for the everyday essentials, or Detailed for everything down to per-core activity, clock speeds and the resolution a game is actually outputting. Each reading can be turned on or off, emphasized, or shown as a number, a bar or a graph.

A 60-second FPS history makes dips easy to spot, temperatures automatically highlight with a warning in the overlay past certain thresholds, and memory pressure has its own history graph. Lay it out as a vertical panel or a horizontal strip, from 0.5× to 2× size.

The HUD never takes focus from the game, and clicks pass straight through it; hold Option to drag it somewhere else. Toggle it with Option-H, even mid-game, or let it hide itself when no frame rate is detected and come back when a game starts rendering.

Press Option-L to log your selected readings to CSV once per second, even with the overlay switched off, and compare settings or games in a spreadsheet afterwards.

Pin a few readings to the menu bar to watch your Mac outside games, even with the HUD switched off. Readings turned off aren’t measured at all, so the overlay only samples what you use.

Display compatibility.

Windowed and borderless (recommended)

The most compatible option; it just works. The HUD stays visible over any game or app, correctly scaled and Retina-sharp regardless of the game’s rendering resolution.

Fullscreen — native macOS games

Generally works. Some games change macOS display scaling in exclusive fullscreen, and since the HUD follows macOS scaling, it can end up smaller or larger along with the rest of the system UI. Setting a game to 4K, for example, can shrink it noticeably. If that happens, adjust Size in the PerformanceHUD menu (0.5× to 2×) until it fits. Confirmed working in exclusive fullscreen in Stray, and in Minecraft with Vulkan rendering.

Fullscreen — Windows games through compatibility layers

With CrossOver or similar tools, the HUD only appears reliably when the game’s fullscreen resolution matches your desktop’s current “looks like” resolution, or its 2× Retina equivalent. Confirmed working at matching resolutions in Red Dead Redemption 2 and Resident Evil Requiem through CrossOver. If the HUD disappears, switch to one of those resolutions or to windowed/borderless.

Finding your desktop resolution

Open System Settings → Displays and hover over the selected scaling thumbnail, or choose Show List. Double both numbers for the 2× Retina equivalent. A 13-inch MacBook Air at its default scaling, for example, uses 1470×956, so RDR2 in fullscreen through CrossOver would need an in-game resolution of 1470×956 or 2940×1912. Windowed and borderless modes don’t need a matching resolution at all.
